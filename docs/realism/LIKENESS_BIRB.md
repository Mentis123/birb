# Birb realism — the likeness rubric

Twenty-eight things that either read or do not, each tied to the image that
settles it. **Acceptance is 26 of 28 (at least 90%), with every contact check
C1–C5 passing.** The contact checks are hard requirements because a floating or
impossible perch breaks the interaction even if the total score is high.

This is the half of the gate that draw-call, triangle and frame-time checks
cannot measure. Score it by eye against matched views. The list is committed
before the art it judges so a finished implementation cannot negotiate its own
target afterwards.

## Reference authority

The four images in [`reference/`](reference/) were generated from scratch and
selected from two candidates per brief. They settle only the visible
relationships named below. They are art-direction evidence, not biological
measurements: do not infer an exact feather count from them.

The root Birb's blue/cyan identity remains fixed. The bluebird references set
anatomy, feather layering, value separation and contact. Their orange and white
plumage are not a request to recolour Birb.

## How to score

Capture four matched views at the same profile, viewport and seed:

1. level flight, near-lateral chase view;
2. perched, three-quarter view from slightly below;
3. the forest river valley from above its canopy;
4. the landmark pine and adjacent forest floor at arm's-length camera distance.

Compare each capture beside its cited reference. A check passes only when its
absence would be obvious at the intended phone view. Score the whole list after
every material, geometry, lighting or camera change.

## A · Bird silhouette and mass

- [ ] **A1** In level flight the bird reads as a compact songbird: rounded chest,
      tapered abdomen and no long cylindrical neck or aircraft-like fuselage.
      *(ref: [`bird-flight-side.png`](reference/bird-flight-side.png))*
- [ ] **A2** The head joins the shoulders as one continuous feathered mass; it
      does not sit on a visible stalk or intersect the torso.
      *(ref: [`bird-flight-side.png`](reference/bird-flight-side.png))*
- [ ] **A3** The bill is short, straight and tapered, projects forward from the
      face, and remains readable without becoming an oversized cone.
      *(ref: [`bird-perched-contact.png`](reference/bird-perched-contact.png))*
- [ ] **A4** The tail leaves the rump as a narrow layered fan with individually
      readable outer edges; it is not one flat wedge or a spray detached from
      the body. *(ref: [`bird-flight-side.png`](reference/bird-flight-side.png))*

## B · Wing construction and feather hierarchy

- [ ] **B1** The outer flight wing resolves into distinct long, tapered primary
      feathers rather than fingers, tubes or one serrated plate.
      *(ref: [`bird-flight-side.png`](reference/bird-flight-side.png))*
- [ ] **B2** A second row of broader, shorter secondaries overlaps the primaries
      closer to the body. The two groups remain distinguishable at chase
      distance. *(ref: [`bird-flight-side.png`](reference/bird-flight-side.png))*
- [ ] **B3** Smaller overlapping coverts bridge the torso and the flight-feather
      rows, so the wing root is layered instead of bare or mechanically hinged.
      *(ref: [`bird-flight-side.png`](reference/bird-flight-side.png))*
- [ ] **B4** Feather overlap runs from body toward wingtip: each visible layer
      sits over the next without reversed shingles or unexplained gaps.
      *(ref: [`bird-flight-side.png`](reference/bird-flight-side.png))*
- [ ] **B5** The leading edge is one continuous shoulder-to-tip curve without a
      detached hand, a pinched elbow or a hard right-angle break.
      *(ref: [`bird-flight-side.png`](reference/bird-flight-side.png))*
- [ ] **B6** Near and far wings read as two coherent planes attached to the same
      upper back; neither passes through the torso or resembles an extra wing.
      *(ref: [`bird-flight-side.png`](reference/bird-flight-side.png))*
- [ ] **B7** When folded, the wing lies against the flank and its long feather
      tips point toward the tail; it does not hover, fan open or end as a single
      shell. *(ref: [`bird-perched-contact.png`](reference/bird-perched-contact.png))*

## C · Feet, perch contact and resting weight — all five required

- [ ] **C1** Exactly two legs emerge from the underside of the body below its
      centre of mass, with no duplicated, missing or side-mounted limb.
      *(ref: [`bird-perched-contact.png`](reference/bird-perched-contact.png))*
- [ ] **C2** Forward toes visibly curl over the near side of the branch instead
      of ending on its top plane. *(ref: [`bird-perched-contact.png`](reference/bird-perched-contact.png))*
- [ ] **C3** At least one opposing rear toe wraps behind the branch, creating a
      credible clamp rather than two parallel hands.
      *(ref: [`bird-perched-contact.png`](reference/bird-perched-contact.png))*
- [ ] **C4** Claw tips meet the bark surface without hovering above it or sinking
      through the branch. *(ref: [`bird-perched-contact.png`](reference/bird-perched-contact.png))*
- [ ] **C5** The resting body settles over the planted feet and the branch: belly,
      folded wing and tail form a stable weight line rather than a flight pose
      frozen above a perch. *(ref: [`bird-perched-contact.png`](reference/bird-perched-contact.png))*

## D · Plumage structure, value and scale

- [ ] **D1** Feather direction follows the form: crown feathers sweep back,
      breast feathers turn down, and wing feathers run toward the tip rather
      than appearing as uniform surface noise.
      *(ref: [`bird-perched-contact.png`](reference/bird-perched-contact.png))*
- [ ] **D2** The fixed blue/cyan palette still forms large readable regions for
      crown, back, wing and underside; detail does not dissolve the bird into
      equal-frequency speckle. *(ref: [`bird-perched-contact.png`](reference/bird-perched-contact.png))*
- [ ] **D3** Close views expose fine feather edges, while chase distance retains
      the primary/secondary/coverts grouping. Detail works at both scales.
      *(ref: [`bird-flight-side.png`](reference/bird-flight-side.png))*
- [ ] **D4** Light rolls across the feather layers and body volume without
      painted outlines, baked highlights or plastic uniform gloss.
      *(ref: [`bird-flight-side.png`](reference/bird-flight-side.png))*

## E · Forest river composition, depth and light

- [ ] **E1** The river forms one continuous winding path from foreground through
      middle distance; it does not read as disconnected blue pools.
      *(ref: [`forest-river-golden-hour.png`](reference/forest-river-golden-hour.png))*
- [ ] **E2** Wooded banks have irregular canopy edges and a readable transition
      through gravel and rock into water, rather than trees ending at a ruler
      line. *(ref: [`forest-river-golden-hour.png`](reference/forest-river-golden-hour.png))*
- [ ] **E3** Exposed far-bank geology forms a distinct layered rock mass that
      holds its shape beside the trees and water.
      *(ref: [`forest-river-golden-hour.png`](reference/forest-river-golden-hour.png))*
- [ ] **E4** Mist occupies low ground in broken pockets and reveals depth; it
      neither blankets the full frame nor becomes opaque white geometry.
      *(ref: [`forest-river-golden-hour.png`](reference/forest-river-golden-hour.png))*
- [ ] **E5** Golden light rakes across crown edges while foreground foliage keeps
      cool shadow detail and distant ridges lose contrast through aerial
      perspective. *(ref: [`forest-river-golden-hour.png`](reference/forest-river-golden-hour.png))*

## F · Bark, litter and wet gravel at reading distance

- [ ] **F1** Pine bark reads as overlapping plates separated by deep mostly
      vertical fissures, with plate sizes credible beside needles and stones.
      *(ref: [`bark-and-ground-arm-length.png`](reference/bark-and-ground-arm-length.png))*
- [ ] **F2** The forest floor contains distinct needles, small twigs, litter and
      irregular gravel at several sizes instead of one repeated noise texture.
      *(ref: [`bark-and-ground-arm-length.png`](reference/bark-and-ground-arm-length.png))*
- [ ] **F3** Wet gravel reads darker and locally smoother than dry needles and
      bark, without a uniform glossy coat across the whole ground.
      *(ref: [`bark-and-ground-arm-length.png`](reference/bark-and-ground-arm-length.png))*
